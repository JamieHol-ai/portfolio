## The problem

Wheel odometry measures how fast the wheels are turning, which is only the same
as how fast the car is moving when the tyres are not slipping. Under braking and
on corner exit they come apart, and the estimator has no independent way to
notice: every velocity input it has is derived from the same wheels.

An optical speed sensor measures against the ground directly, so its velocity
estimate is unaffected by slip. That makes it the one input that can contradict
the encoders rather than agree with them by construction.

## Approach

The sensor talks over CAN, so the work split into three parts: getting frames
off the bus and decoded, wrapping that in a template other subsystems could
reuse for future CAN devices, and handing the resulting velocity to the state
estimator as a measurement with its own noise model rather than as a drop-in
replacement for wheel speed.

The reusable template mattered more than the sensor did. The team adds CAN
devices regularly and each one had previously been integrated from scratch.

## What I took from it

Most of the difficulty was not the protocol. It was working out, with people who
had been on the team longer than I had, which of the existing assumptions in the
estimation stack the new measurement was allowed to break — and then explaining
the change clearly enough that it could be reviewed by someone who had not been
reading the datasheet all week.

## What I'd change

The measurement is currently trusted at a fixed covariance. The sensor's own
quality flag degrades over surface changes and standing water, and folding that
into the noise model is the obvious next step — it is the difference between a
sensor that helps most of the time and one the filter can rely on.
