## The problem

PLACEHOLDER COPY. Wheel odometry is dead reckoning through a model of the tyre,
and the model assumes the contact patch is not sliding. On a Formula Student
track that assumption fails in the two places the estimator most needs to be
right: under braking into a hairpin, and on corner exit with the inside wheel
unloaded. The error is not noise — it is a bias that appears whenever the car is
doing something interesting, and integrates.

The optical speed sensor removed part of this, giving a ground-referenced
longitudinal velocity that does not care about slip. What it does not give is
rotation, or lateral motion. For those the estimator was still trusting a model.

## Approach

PLACEHOLDER COPY. LiDAR odometry estimates the rigid-body transform between
successive scans directly from the geometry: whatever moved the points is what
moved the car, and no part of that reasoning involves the tyre.

The scan-to-scan estimate is the cheap part. Making it useful over a lap is not:
consecutive-frame registration accumulates error monotonically, so the interesting
design questions are all about what the current scan is registered *against*, and
how much history that reference carries.

The general shape of the problem, for the write-up to fill in: motion distortion
within a scan while the car is moving fast enough for the sweep to matter;
degenerate geometry where the track is locally straight and featureless, so the
along-track direction is poorly constrained; and the cost of a registration that
does not converge inside the time budget on a target that has to keep up in real
time.

## Where it goes

This is not meant to be run open-loop. It exists to be the second measurement
input to the [iterated EKF](/log/iterated-ekf/), which was deliberately built on
wheelspeeds and IMU alone first — so switching this on gives a before-and-after
on the same dataset rather than a separate experiment.

The two error characteristics are complementary, which is the whole argument for
fusing them: wheel odometry is smooth and biased, LiDAR odometry is unbiased and
occasionally very wrong. A filter that knows which is which should beat both.
